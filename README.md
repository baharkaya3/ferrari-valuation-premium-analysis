# Ferrari Valuation Premium Analysis

### Dual-Cohort Comparable Company Analysis
**Automotive vs. Ultra-Luxury**

*2023–2025 Financial & Relative Valuation Study*

</div>

---

> **Research Question**
>
> Is Ferrari priced primarily as an automotive manufacturer, or does its valuation reflect the economics of an ultra-luxury brand?

---

## Executive Snapshot

| Metric | Ferrari 2025 | Automotive Cohort | Ultra-Luxury Cohort |
|:---|---:|---:|---:|
| **EBITDA Margin** | **38.8%** | 16%–26% | 28%–48% |
| **EV/EBITDA** | **20.9x** | 6x–9x | 18x–33x |
| **Positioning** | **Luxury-aligned** | Automotive | Ultra-Luxury |

### Core Finding

Ferrari's 2025 profitability and valuation profile is substantially closer to the selected ultra-luxury cohort than to traditional automotive peers.

---

## Peer Group Construction

The analysis evaluates Ferrari against two distinct peer cohorts.

| Cohort | Companies |
|:---|:---|
| **Automotive** | Porsche AG · Aston Martin |
| **Ultra-Luxury** | Hermès · Ferretti Group · Brunello Cucinelli |
| **Target** | **Ferrari N.V.** |

---

## Key Findings

### 01 — Profitability

The automotive cohort generated EBITDA margins of approximately **16%–26%**, while the ultra-luxury cohort generally operated within a higher **28%–48%** range.

Ferrari reported an EBITDA margin of **38.8% in 2025**, placing it well above the traditional automotive cohort and within the profitability range observed among the selected luxury companies.

### 02 — Valuation Premium

Traditional automotive peers traded at approximately **6x–9x EV/EBITDA**, while the selected ultra-luxury companies generally commanded higher valuation multiples.

Ferrari's **2025 EV/EBITDA multiple of approximately 20.9x** places it substantially above the traditional automotive cohort and closer to the valuation framework observed among ultra-luxury companies.

### 03 — Profitability–Valuation Relationship

The relationship between EBITDA Margin and EV/EBITDA was examined across the two cohorts.

The ultra-luxury cohort produced a strong positive relationship with an **R² of 0.947**.

Ferrari's positioning is closer to the luxury cohort's valuation–profitability relationship than to the traditional automotive group.

## Methodology

### Comparable Company Analysis

The study applies a **Dual-Cohort Comparable Company Analysis** to evaluate Ferrari's relative valuation.

The analysis compares:

**Automotive Economics**

Porsche AG · Aston Martin

**Ultra-Luxury Economics**

Hermès · Ferretti Group · Brunello Cucinelli

### Financial Metrics

The primary metrics used in the analysis are:

- Revenue
- Gross Profit
- Gross Profit Margin
- EBITDA
- EBITDA Margin
- Enterprise Value
- EV/EBITDA

---

## Currency Normalization

Because the selected companies report in different currencies, financial data were standardized to **EUR**.

- Income statement items such as Revenue and EBITDA were converted using annual average exchange rates.
- Balance sheet items such as Cash and Debt were converted using year-end spot exchange rates.
- Market capitalization was converted using the relevant year-end exchange rate.

This approach improves comparability across companies and reporting periods.

---

## Key Analytical Insight

The results suggest that Ferrari's market valuation cannot be explained solely by conventional automotive manufacturing economics.

Its combination of:

- High operating margins
- Pricing power
- Brand differentiation
- Limited production volumes
- Controlled supply
- Premium valuation multiples

is more consistent with the economic characteristics of an **ultra-luxury brand**.

The findings are consistent with a **Veblen-like brand economics hypothesis**, although the analysis does not establish a direct causal Veblen effect.

## Limitations

**Ferretti Group**  
Although Ferretti operates in the ultra-luxury yacht segment, its valuation multiple is substantially lower than those of some other luxury companies. This highlights differences in manufacturing intensity and business models within the luxury sector.

**Aston Martin**  
Significant EBITDA volatility affects its EV/EBITDA multiple across the analysis period and introduces noise into the automotive cohort.

**Sample Size**  
The peer groups contain a relatively small number of companies, meaning individual company characteristics can materially affect cohort-level observations.

**Causality**  
The analysis identifies relationships between profitability and valuation but does not establish that Veblen dynamics directly cause Ferrari's valuation premium.

---

## Conclusion

Ferrari's profitability and valuation characteristics are more closely aligned with the selected ultra-luxury cohort than with traditional automotive peers.

The combination of **38.8% EBITDA margin** and **20.9x EV/EBITDA in 2025** illustrates Ferrari's distinctive position at the intersection of automotive manufacturing and ultra-luxury consumer economics.

The evidence supports interpreting Ferrari as a company whose valuation is influenced not only by manufacturing economics, but also by **brand equity, pricing power and controlled scarcity**.
